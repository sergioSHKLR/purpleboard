# Privacy

*Last updated: 20 November 2026*

DEADBOARD (the website, the app on iPhone and iPad, and the desktop app) is made by Tibor Holtman.

**What DEADBOARD collects: anonymous visit counts on the website; nothing in the apps, unless you
send feedback.**

- No account, no sign-in, no ads, no cookies, no cross-site tracking.
- The iPhone, iPad and desktop apps have no analytics and no third-party SDKs.
- Your progress, settings, imported boards and installed mods stay on your device (browser storage
  on the website, the app's own storage in the apps). They are never uploaded.
- Boards and files you import are read on your device only.

**Feedback.** If you use the Feedback button and press Send, your message, the contact you chose to
give (optional) and, if you left the box ticked, technical details (app version, browser / system
version, screen size, graphics card, the open board) are sent to the developer by email through
FormSubmit (formsubmit.co). With the box ticked a bug report also carries a compressed snapshot of
the bench, so the developer can rebuild what you saw: the changes you made to the open board (only
for boards that come with DEADBOARD), power and meter settings, the readings on screen, and the
last few messages and errors the app showed. A board you imported yourself is never sent: the
snapshot only says what kind of file it was and how many parts it has. You see a summary of the
technical details before sending. They are used only to answer you and fix the problem, and
deleted when no longer needed. Ask for deletion at any time by
replying to the developer's answer, or through the contact below.

**Website visit statistics.** The website (not the apps) counts visits with Umami Cloud
(umami.is), which uses no cookies and stores no IP addresses or personal data. For each visit it
records the page, the referring site, the country, the browser, the operating system, the device type
and the screen size. It also records how long you used the bench, as milestones such as "active 5
min". Active time only counts while the tab is visible and you have used the mouse, keyboard or screen
in the last two minutes. On the bench it also records which of the boards that come with DEADBOARD
you open and fix, when you generate a new problem on one, and when you finish the tutorial. To see where
new players get stuck, it also records whether the bench's graphics started (and which kind, on a
phone or a computer; when the bench is slow to start or fails, at which start-up step), how far the first lesson's guide was followed or where it was closed, which
tool was used first, and whether the first test run on each board passed (a board you imported or made yourself is only
counted as "custom", never by name). When you leave the homepage, it records roughly how long it was on screen (as a
range such as "5–15 s"), how far you scrolled (in quarters), whether you went to the bench, another page or another
site, and whether the 3D board was shown. When you leave the bench within its first minute, it records the same time
range and how far you got (loading, the bench, a tool, a test, a fix, the tutorial step). If the bench hits a
program error, it records the error's type and which tool and built-in board were in use, never the error's text. Umami combines this into totals, so the developer can see roughly how
many people use DEADBOARD and for how long. You can't be identified, and you aren't followed across
sites or from one month to the next. If your browser sends Do Not Track, nothing is counted.
Content blockers usually block it too.

**Submitting a mod.** If you press **Submit** on a mod in the Mods window and send it, the mod
(its `.zip`, or the link you give), your note and the contact you chose to give (optional) are sent
to the developer by email through FormSubmit, like feedback. They are used to review the mod and
answer you. If the mod is accepted it is published on deadboard.org under its licence, with the
author name in its `mod.json`; your note and contact are never published.

**Community mods (deadboard.org).** The **Community** tab of the Mods window loads the list of mods
from deadboard.org and, when you press Install, downloads that mod from deadboard.org. Nothing about
you is sent.

**Community mods (mod.io).** The Mods window can list and install mods that players publish on
mod.io. This happens only when you open its **Browse mod.io** tab: the app then asks
mod.io for the list of DEADBOARD mods, loads their pictures and, when you press Install, downloads
that mod. Like any website, mod.io sees your IP address and browser for these requests; you don't
sign in and DEADBOARD sends nothing about you. See mod.io's privacy policy (https://mod.io/privacy)
and terms (https://mod.io/terms). Installed mods are kept on your device like a mod you add
yourself. On the website, visit statistics (below) count which mod.io mod was installed, by the
mod's public name.

**Links.** Links to GitHub, the modding guide or board sources open in your browser; those sites
have their own privacy policies.

**Website hosting.** The website is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses
for security (see GitHub's privacy statement). The apps contact no server unless you send feedback or open the
Browse mod.io tab.

**Children.** DEADBOARD is suitable for all ages and does not knowingly collect data from anyone.

**Contact:** https://github.com/tholtman1-del/deadboard/issues (or the in-app Feedback button).
